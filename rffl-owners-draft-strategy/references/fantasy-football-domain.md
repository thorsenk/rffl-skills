# Fantasy Football Domain Reference

## Canonical terms

- **Non-PPR:** Receptions score zero points. Often called standard scoring, but "non-PPR" is more precise.
- **Half-PPR:** Each reception scores 0.5 points unless custom rules say otherwise.
- **PPR:** Each reception scores 1.0 point unless custom rules say otherwise.
- **TE premium:** Tight ends receive more points per reception or first down than other positions.
- **ADP:** Average Draft Position. It describes market behavior, not the recommended rank.
- **ECR:** Expert Consensus Rankings. It summarizes expert recommendations.
- **AAV:** Average Auction Value. It describes observed salary-cap prices.
- **Projection:** Expected fantasy output under a defined scoring system.
- **Positional rank:** Order within one player position.
- **Overall rank:** One cross-position draft order.
- **Tier:** A group of players with similar draft value and a meaningful drop after the group.
- **FLEX:** A roster slot, not a player position. Standard FLEX usually permits RB, WR, or TE.
- **Superflex:** A roster slot that usually permits QB, RB, WR, or TE.
- **Keeper:** A player retained from a prior season at a defined cost.
- **Live selection:** A pick the Team must actively make. It can be lower than board rounds when keepers prefill cells.
- **Handcuff:** A backup whose value is strongly tied to a specific starter's availability.
- **Stack:** Two or more players whose scoring outcomes are positively correlated, usually QB plus pass catcher.
- **Value cliff:** A meaningful drop in expected draft value after a player or tier.

## League factors that materially change rankings

- scoring per reception
- TE premium
- passing touchdown value
- interception penalties
- bonuses for long plays or yardage thresholds
- points per first down
- return-yard scoring
- number of Teams
- starting roster depth
- FLEX and Superflex count
- position limits
- bench depth
- best-ball versus managed lineup
- keeper costs and unavailable players
- draft platform and room-specific ADP

## Position-specific considerations

### Quarterback

QB scarcity is highly format-dependent. One-QB rankings usually discount replacement value. Superflex and 2QB formats sharply increase QB value. Passing-TD scoring, rushing production, and league size can create major tier cliffs.

### Running back

Consider projected workload, receiving role, goal-line role, offensive environment, durability, and contingency value. Non-PPR usually increases the relative value of rushing volume and touchdowns.

### Wide receiver

Consider target share, route participation, quarterback quality, touchdown role, and target competition. PPR formats increase the value of high-volume reception profiles.

### Tight end

Consider route rate, target share, red-zone role, and replacement-level drop. TE premium can move the position materially higher overall.

### DST and kicker

These positions are highly sensitive to late news, schedule, and league scoring. Do not devote early draft capital unless league settings create an unusual edge. Rankings should be current close to draft day.

### IDP

IDP rankings are unusable without exact scoring and roster requirements. Tackle-heavy scoring, big-play scoring, and position classification can produce different boards.

## Strategy concepts

Treat named strategies as descriptive patterns, not mandatory identities:

- anchor/hero RB
- zero RB
- robust RB
- WR-heavy
- elite QB
- late-round QB
- elite TE
- late-round TE
- stars-and-scrubs salary-cap build
- balanced salary-cap build

The final board should remain value-aware. A strategy can guide tie-breaking and roster construction, but it should not justify a severe reach without explicit user approval.
